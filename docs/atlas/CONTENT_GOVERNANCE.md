# Darma Tech Atlas Content Governance

## Purpose

The Tech Atlas helps people discover reliable references, learn through practical paths, understand technology careers, compare ways of working, and recognize common team terminology. The content is educational and practical; it is not a paid directory or a ranking marketplace.

## Source hierarchy

Use this order when selecting factual references:

1. Official documentation, standards, specifications, or framework owners.
2. Primary maintainers, original research, or recognized professional bodies.
3. High-quality educational material with clear authorship and maintenance.
4. Community explanations when they add practical context not available in primary material.

A community source must not replace an available primary source for a basic factual claim without a clear reason.

## Acceptance criteria for resources

A resource should:

- solve a recognizable technical, design, learning, or delivery need;
- have a stable canonical HTTPS URL;
- add value not already covered by an equivalent catalog entry;
- disclose its ownership and pricing only when those facts are verified;
- avoid deceptive downloads, forced browser notifications, harmful scripts, or misleading claims;
- have enough original value to justify sending a Darma user away from the site.

Commercial resources may be included when genuinely useful, but payment does not create priority and affiliate links are not accepted.

## Content states

- **Verified:** factual fields and the canonical URL were reviewed against reliable evidence.
- **Review needed:** the resource may be useful, but one or more important facts remain unverified.
- **Archived:** retained for historical or migration reasons but not recommended as an active starting point.

A successful automated response does not by itself make content verified. Human review remains necessary for quality, relevance, ownership, and learning value.

## Review cadence

- Featured and path-critical sources: review at least every 180 days.
- Other verified sources: review at least annually.
- Review-needed sources: improve when touched or when a community report provides evidence.
- Broken or misleading sources: correct, replace, or archive as soon as confirmed.

## Learning path governance

A learning path must have:

- a defined audience and prerequisite level;
- ordered stages with a clear reason for their sequence;
- a practical checkpoint for every stage;
- projects with inspectable outputs;
- primary references for important technical claims;
- no promise that completing the path guarantees employment.

New tools should not be added merely because they are popular. They should support a stage outcome or an explicit alternative.

## Career and organization governance

Career guides describe common patterns, not universal job contracts. Titles vary by company, country, team size, and product model. Seniority should be explained through scope, autonomy, judgment, communication, and impact—not only years of experience.

Team models and ways of working must include limitations and healthy-use signals. Darma should not present Agile, Scrum, Kanban, DevOps, Waterfall, circles, squads, or any other model as automatically superior.

## Editorial style

- Use direct language and concrete examples.
- Define specialist terms when first introduced.
- Separate evidence from Darma's interpretation.
- Avoid exaggerated claims such as “best,” “complete,” or “industry standard” without evidence.
- Prefer inclusive examples that do not assume one geography, company size, or educational background.
- Explain what a user can do next.

## Logos and trademarks

Prefer locally stored assets from official brand kits or clearly licensed icon libraries. A logo is used only to identify its source and does not imply endorsement. Do not create an imitation logo that could be mistaken for the official mark.

## Removal and correction

A resource may be removed or archived when it becomes unsafe, misleading, abandoned, duplicated, inaccessible, or outside the project scope. Corrections should preserve useful history in Git rather than hiding why a decision changed.
